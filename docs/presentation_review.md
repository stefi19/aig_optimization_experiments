## Corrections and interpretation

- The ranking plot's universal-sounding synthesis-lineage title now explicitly
  describes controlled ancestor labels. These are disclosed algebraic labels,
  not instrumented real synthesis histories. Width and flow holdouts reuse
  families/designs; four of each split's 24 ranking queries lack an ancestor.
- The 10/24 structural versus 6/24 hybrid/contextual versus 4/24 global result
  is a negative result for the motivating ranking expectation on this suite.
  Feature ablations do not justify a separate observability-ranking improvement.
- The actual rare-observable and reconvergent-mask BLIF pairs supply diagrams,
  exact counts, weighted tables, and replayed witnesses. A source ancestor is
  not automatically a safe replacement in its new consumer role.
- Tight-budget ordering is preliminary: 18 selected target IDs, repeated seeds,
  one distinct accepted graph shared across two flow instances, an interval
  including zero, unchanged full-budget recovery, and no measured speedup.
  Full proposal truth vectors already exist. Exact hashing is an essential
  untested baseline; fewer vector comparisons are not end-to-end savings.
- The output-0 BLIF correction and 32-row audit are explained without implying
  that all 2,609 historical rows or old fingerprint tables were revalidated.
- The next checkpoint and source-cut studies are explicitly proposed. The
  protocol includes independent provenance, disjoint family holdout, strong
  exact baselines, total costs, deduplication, and supporting/falsifying outcomes.

## Speaking guide

The sequence is problem → approximate-computing connection → motivating
expectations → exact methods and audit → experiments → unexpected findings →
interpretation → proposed experiments. Every slide has speaker notes available
with **N** or **Notes**, including scope, denominators, and proof distinctions.
[All speaker notes](presentation/speaker_notes.md) are also available as a single
readable document. The final three Carmine slides work as a short standalone answer. The last slide
links to the updated PDF, case-study JSON, research ledger, and historical deck.

Global distance, contextual error, and historical ancestry are three separate
questions. Use the ODC truth table to explain why averaging disagreement and
observability separately is wrong. In the rare case, do not treat the six-input
conjunction as a fair independent bit: its true probability is 1/64.

The report retains references to Vašíček (formal error analysis), Hoeffding,
ABC, Z3, DAG-aware rewriting, and don't-care resynthesis. No new novelty claim is
made for these established techniques. The strongest open research question is
whether approximation saves total cost beyond strong exact alternatives on
unseen design families.
