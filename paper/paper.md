# Abstract

This artifact studies when internal correspondences can be recovered after
logic synthesis and when they cannot. The central contribution is not a single
large recovery count: it is an auditable evidence discipline that separates
controlled graph-active successes, blind bounded semantic recovery, exact
locality certificates, oracle diagnostics, and historical ineligible rows. The
repository includes reproducible checkers, pinned ABC validation, Z3-backed
proof paths, and derived paper tables that map every headline claim to concrete
CSV rows.

# Problem

Logic synthesis intentionally rewrites internal structure. Names, local
topology, support sets, and even apparent semantic signatures can drift under
passes such as rewriting, refactoring, resubstitution, and don't-care
optimization. This makes internal source mapping and debug correspondence
fragile even when whole-design equivalence is easy to prove.

The artifact asks a narrower question: under which evidence conditions can a
candidate internal correspondence be counted as recovered, and which blockers
explain null results?

# Threat Model

The artifact treats denominators as part of the scientific claim. Controlled
generated BLIF rows, blind generated BLIF rows, generated research targets,
historical diagnostics, oracle rows, and future external RTL rows are never
merged unless a table explicitly says so. A graph rewrite is counted only when
the row has graph activity and global equivalence evidence. Diagnostic exact
minimum certificates are valuable, but they are not counted as recovered
boundaries unless a validated graph artifact exists.

# Method

The repository implements four connected proof paths.

1. Source-side counterpart construction selects graph-active source expressions
   and proves local equivalence before synthesizing a quotient.
2. Cross-netlist transplantation builds input and output adapters and requires
   local proof plus whole-design CEC before accepting a transplant.
3. Blind semantic CEGIS enumerates bounded template candidates and records
   concrete counterexamples whenever a candidate is refuted.
4. Necessity-first and formal locality audits explain why historical failures
   remain failures: missing provenance, target irrelevance, non-compact exact
   interfaces, or absent validated rewrite artifacts.

# Evidence Summary

The canonical paper-facing table is
`results/research_wow/recoverability_frontier.csv`. It records:

- 10/10 controlled graph-active source-counterpart rewrites.
- 12/12 controlled cross-netlist transplants.
- 3/24 blind parametric CEGIS regions formally verified.
- 31/48 necessity-first generated targets with compact exact input interfaces.
- 0/48 necessity-first generated targets with validated graph rewrite artifacts.
- 26/56 historical formal-locality diagnostic rows with exact input minima.
- 0/56 corrected historical cross-netlist rows counted as graph-active
  recoveries.

# Failure Taxonomy

The failure taxonomy in `results/research_wow/failure_taxonomy.csv` converts
null results into auditable blocker classes. The largest classes are missing
optimized artifacts, target irrelevance after reconstruction, non-compact exact
interfaces, and absent validated rewrite artifacts. These categories prevent a
reviewer from mistaking historical or diagnostic rows for failed executions of
the final graph-rewrite contract.

# Demo Case Study

`make demo-wow` prints a reviewer-safe trace that combines a controlled
positive proof path with a blind counterexample-refinement trace. The controlled
case proves a graph-active source counterpart, decomposes the source window,
synthesizes an exact quotient, and checks source/cross equivalence with ABC. The
blind case shows two concrete SAT counterexamples before bounded candidate
exhaustion, plus a separate blind positive control with an exhaustive formal
proof.

# Ablations and Baselines

The derived ablation and baseline summaries in `results/research_wow/` show
that successful recovery depends on the proof-carrying construction and on
strict provenance filtering. Baselines that produce local or diagnostic
evidence are kept separate from graph-active recovery counts.

# Limitations

External RTL claims remain out of scope until a pinned redistributable RTL
corpus and Yosys lowering flow are committed. Oracle rows are diagnostic only.
Approximate or sampled evidence remains approximate or sampled evidence. Exact
locality certificates do not imply graph-active rewrites without a validated
artifact and whole-design CEC.

# Related Work Positioning

The work is adjacent to equivalence checking, SAT sweeping and FRAIG, internal
signal correspondence, observability don't-care reasoning, source mapping, and
formal artifact evaluation. The artifact's main distinction is its claim
discipline: each result is labeled by evidence level, denominator class, and
checker command, so positive and negative results are both reproducible.
